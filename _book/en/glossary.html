Professional terms used in the book, with their English originals. Each entry is linked from its first use in every chapter.

  • AdamW AdamW 优化器
    First occurrence: Chapter 4 · Scaling Laws and Compute Allocation

    AdamW is the default, with decoupled weight decay and two moment estimates whose state is the dominant memory term, which is why Chapter 9 shards it.

  • adversarial robustness 对抗鲁棒性
    First occurrence: Chapter 43 · Adversarial Robustness and Red-Teaming

    That search, and the effort to defeat it, now has a stable shape: four families of jailbreak exploit different surfaces, red-teaming is a measurement discipline rather than a one-time audit, every known defense is partial, and adversarial robustness is a property of the system rather than of the model alone.

  • application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) 专用集成电路
    First occurrence: Chapter 48 · The Compute Frontier: Bandwidth, Not FLOPs

    The custom-application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) bet is vertical integration: co-design the chip with the network and skip the merchant margin.

  • arithmetic coding 算术编码
    First occurrence: Chapter 3 · Borrowed Ideas: What AI Took From Other Sciences

    The correspondence is precise rather than poetic: for any distribution there is a prefix code of length $\lceil -\log P\rceil$, and any prefix code defines a distribution, an identity Grünwald calls the most important observation behind the whole theory, and arithmetic coding realizes it as a stream to within a couple of bits of the entropy.

  • attention sink 注意力汇
    First occurrence: Chapter 26 · Structured and Long-Context Inference

    Long-context serving decides which keys and values survive, from sliding windows, attention sink behavior, and heavy-hitter eviction to newer query-aware policies.

  • automatic speech recognition (ASR) 自动语音识别
    First occurrence: Chapter 12 · Speech and Realtime Voice

    automatic speech recognition (ASR) maps a long, unsegmented waveform, hundreds of frames, to a short transcript, with no per-frame label given.

  • autoregression 自回归
    First occurrence: Chapter 11 · Non-Autoregressive and Diffusion Language Models

    autoregression is serial: generating $n$ tokens takes $n$ sequential forward passes, each waiting on the last.

  • BM25 BM25 稀疏检索
    First occurrence: Chapter 33 · RAG and Retrieval

    Karpukhin et al., in Dense Passage Retrieval, showed that a dual-encoder trained on a few thousand question-passage pairs beats a strong BM25 baseline by a wide margin on top-20 retrieval accuracy across open-domain QA.

  • Brussels effect 布鲁塞尔效应
    First occurrence: Chapter 45 · Law, Regulation, and Policy

    Because of the dynamic Anu Bradford named the Brussels effect: when a large market regulates, a global firm often finds it cheaper to build one product to the strictest applicable standard than to maintain a patchwork, so the strict standard quietly becomes the default everywhere.

  • budget forcing 预算强制
    First occurrence: Chapter 21 · The Inference-Time Scaling Paradigm

    s1 shows the lever in its barest form with budget forcing, a decoding-time intervention that controls how long the model thinks by suppressing the end-of-thinking token and appending the word "Wait" to push the model to continue, or by forcing termination to cap the spend.

  • Byzantine fault tolerance 拜占庭容错
    First occurrence: Chapter 38 · Evaluating Agents and Capabilities

    They co-hallucinate, share blind spots, and lean toward the same fluent-but-wrong answers on the same inputs, so a third copy does not buy the fault diversity that Byzantine fault tolerance promises.

  • canary strings 金丝雀字符串
    First occurrence: Chapter 36 · Benchmarks and Their Discontents

    Embedded canary strings, unique markers placed in a held-out set, reveal that the set was ingested if the model can reproduce them.

  • capital expense (CapEx) 资本性支出
    First occurrence: Chapter 56 · The Economics of AI

    Training is a capital expense (CapEx).

  • chain of thought (CoT) 思维链
    First occurrence: Chapter 19 · Eliciting Reasoning

    A chain of thought (CoT) helps because it gives intermediate work tokens to live in; sampling many chains and voting helps because wrong paths scatter; trees and searches pay only when the problem has useful branches; and a verifier is the component that turns best-of-N from raw sampling into selection.

  • chip-on-wafer-on-substrate CoWoS
    First occurrence: Chapter 48 · The Compute Frontier: Bandwidth, Not FLOPs

    Modern accelerators are several compute chiplets and a row of HBM stacks tiled side by side on a silicon interposer, the chip-on-wafer-on-substrate package.

  • classifier-free guidance (CFG) 无分类器引导
    First occurrence: Chapter 10 · Diffusion and Flow Matching

    Classifier guidance steers a sample with the gradient of a separately trained noised classifier, and classifier-free guidance (CFG) removes that classifier by training one model both with and without the conditioning signal and extrapolating between them at sampling,

  • compute-optimal 计算最优
    First occurrence: Chapter 4 · Scaling Laws and Compute Allocation

    You fit the constants on runs you can afford, then read off the compute-optimal $N$ and $D$ for the budget you intend to spend.

  • connectionist temporal classification (CTC) 连接主义时序分类
    First occurrence: Chapter 12 · Speech and Realtime Voice

    connectionist temporal classification (CTC) introduces a blank token and sums the probability over every frame-level path that collapses to the target, computed by a forward-backward dynamic program, but it assumes the outputs are conditionally independent given the audio, so it carries no internal language model and leans on an external one.

  • constrained decoding 约束解码
    First occurrence: Chapter 26 · Structured and Long-Context Inference

    Track one: the constrained decoding loop

  • contamination 污染
    First occurrence: Chapter 36 · Benchmarks and Their Discontents

    A held-out set is a pipeline contract, benchmark families saturate, train/test contamination silently inflates numbers, and a score counts as evidence only when the harness, data contract, and uncertainty are visible.

  • continuous batching 连续批处理
    First occurrence: Chapter 22 · The Serving Problem

    This idea, widely called continuous batching, is the foundation every modern engine builds on.

  • contrastive language-image pretraining (CLIP) 对比语言-图像预训练
    First occurrence: Chapter 13 · Multimodal Models: Fusion and Generation

    contrastive language-image pretraining (CLIP) contrastively aligns an image encoder and a text encoder over 400 million pairs into a single embedding space with a softmax InfoNCE loss over an in-batch similarity matrix, so an image can be classified zero-shot by matching it against text prompts, and that aligned space is the conditioning signal much of image generation later reuses.

  • cross-encoder 交叉编码器
    First occurrence: Chapter 33 · RAG and Retrieval

    A cross-encoder reranker feeds the query and chunk together through a transformer and scores their joint representation, which is far more accurate and far too slow to run over the whole corpus.

  • cross-entropy 交叉熵
    First occurrence: Chapter 4 · Scaling Laws and Compute Allocation

    next-token prediction is maximum likelihood over a token stream, cross-entropy on the shifted sequence under a causal mask, so every position is a training example and the corpus is its own supervision.

  • datasheet 数据表
    First occurrence: Chapter 45 · Law, Regulation, and Policy

    The datasheet does the same for a dataset, recording its composition, collection process, and intended uses so a later user can judge its fitness.

  • deceptive alignment 欺骗性对齐
    First occurrence: Chapter 40 · Scalable Oversight and Control

    The answer is deceptive alignment.

  • decode 解码
    First occurrence: Chapter 22 · The Serving Problem

    This is the decode phase: each step feeds the single most recent token through the network to produce the next one, a sequence of thin matrix-vector multiplications.

  • decontamination 去污染
    First occurrence: Chapter 5 · Data Curation and Quality

    The decontamination report is consumed by evaluation in Chapter 36, which supplies the eval registry this chapter checks against.

  • denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM) 去噪扩散概率模型
    First occurrence: Chapter 10 · Diffusion and Flow Matching

    The surprise of denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM) is that the variational bound on this reverse process collapses, once the time-dependent weights are dropped, to an ordinary regression: predict the noise $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$ that was added at a randomly chosen step,

  • differential privacy (DP) 差分隐私
    First occurrence: Chapter 44 · Privacy, Provenance, and Unlearning

    differential privacy (DP) bounds how much any single training record can change the trained model, by adding calibrated noise so that the output distribution is nearly identical whether or not that record was present.

  • diffusion 扩散
    First occurrence: Chapter 10 · Diffusion and Flow Matching

    diffusion and its flow matching generalization are the generative principle behind almost all non-text media, and they are now reaching back into text.

  • diffusion transformer (DiT) 扩散 Transformer
    First occurrence: Chapter 10 · Diffusion and Flow Matching

    The backbone caught up with the rest of the book last: diffusion transformer (DiT) replaces the U-Net with a transformer over latent patches and finds sample quality improving as compute rises, putting diffusion on the same scaling curve as everything else.

  • direct preference optimization (DPO) 直接偏好优化
    First occurrence: Chapter 17 · Direct Preference Optimization and Its Variants

    Kahneman-Tversky optimization (KTO) drops the paired-data requirement. direct preference optimization (DPO) needs a chosen and a rejected response for the same prompt; KTO learns from unpaired binary good or bad labels by scoring each response against a reference point with a prospect-theory utility, the same human-value-of-gains-and-losses shape Kahneman and Tversky used.

  • dual-encoder 双编码器
    First occurrence: Chapter 33 · RAG and Retrieval

    A dual-encoder embeds the query and each chunk into the same vector space, trained so that a question and its answer land near each other even when they share no words.

  • dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) 动态随机存取存储器
    First occurrence: Chapter 23 · Memory and Scheduling

    Mooncake centers the whole architecture on the key-value cache, pooling CPU dynamic random-access memory (DRAM) and SSD across the cluster into a disaggregated cache that prefill writes and decode reads.

  • effective training time ratio (ETTR) 有效训练时间比
    First occurrence: Chapter 51 · The Machine That Breaks at Scale

    The metric that captures this is the effective training time ratio (ETTR), the fraction of wall-clock time the job spends making real progress rather than recovering.

  • evolution strategies 进化策略
    First occurrence: Chapter 3 · Borrowed Ideas: What AI Took From Other Sciences

    Evolution is similar from the other side: evolution strategies optimize a model by perturbing its parameters and keeping what scores well, and Salimans et al. are explicit that this is a class of black-box optimization, an alternative to gradient-based reinforcement learning, with nothing depending on living systems.

  • fertility 繁殖度
    First occurrence: Chapter 11 · Non-Autoregressive and Diffusion Language Models

    Gu's fix was a fertility latent, an integer per source token saying how many target tokens it spawns, which both picks a single mode and answers the length question that autoregression gets for free by emitting an end-of-sequence token.

  • finite-state machine (FSM) 有限状态机
    First occurrence: Chapter 26 · Structured and Long-Context Inference

    Structured output masks logits against a finite-state machine (FSM), then makes that mask cheap through a precomputed index and jump-forward over forced spans.

  • FlashAttention FlashAttention 注意力内核
    First occurrence: Chapter 7 · Transformer Architecture and Its Variants

    FlashAttention belongs to this story only as a reminder of what these variants do not solve.

  • flow matching 流匹配
    First occurrence: Chapter 10 · Diffusion and Flow Matching

    diffusion and its flow matching generalization are the generative principle behind almost all non-text media, and they are now reaching back into text.

  • fully sharded data parallel (FSDP) 全分片数据并行
    First occurrence: Chapter 9 · Training at Scale: Stability and Distributed Parallelism

    Zero Redundancy Optimizer (ZeRO) observed that the optimizer state, gradients, and parameters in standard DP are redundantly replicated, and sharded them across the DP group in three progressive stages; ZeRO-Infinity extended the idea to offload onto CPU and NVMe, and PyTorch fully sharded data parallel (FSDP) made sharded DP a native, ergonomic primitive.

  • gateway 网关
    First occurrence: Chapter 57 · Choosing a Model

    Put a gateway in front so you can route per task and fail over, and re-evaluate the default every quarter; the gap between the top few is small and the versions churn.

  • generative reward model (GenRM) 生成式奖励模型
    First occurrence: Chapter 21 · The Inference-Time Scaling Paradigm

    Rather than a frozen scalar head, generative reward model (GenRM) trains the verifier with the same next-token objective as the generator, jointly on verification and generation, so the verifier can write a chain of thought about a candidate and even spend its own test-time compute by voting over several such judgments.

  • Goodhart's law 古德哈特定律
    First occurrence: Chapter 37 · Judging and Holistic Evaluation

    A number that everyone can see is a number everyone optimizes, and Goodhart's law takes it from there: the measure stops measuring.

  • goodput 有效吞吐量
    First occurrence: Chapter 22 · The Serving Problem

    The objective that survives contact with reality is goodput: the rate of requests completed within their latency targets.

  • gradient clipping 梯度裁剪
    First occurrence: Chapter 4 · Scaling Laws and Compute Allocation

    The stability tricks are z-loss, an auxiliary penalty on the log-partition that stops output logits from drifting; query-key normalization (QK-norm), normalizing query and key before the dot product to cap attention-logit growth; depth-scaled initialization; and global-norm gradient clipping as the blunt safety net.

  • graphics processing unit (GPU) 图形处理器
    First occurrence: Chapter 46 · Accelerators and Networking

    The same byte that would cross a switch on a graphics processing unit (GPU) cluster travels to a physical neighbor on a TPU pod, which is why bandwidth there scales with locality.

  • Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG) 贪婪坐标梯度
    First occurrence: Chapter 43 · Adversarial Robustness and Red-Teaming

    The Greedy Coordinate Gradient (GCG) attack treats jailbreaking as a search problem: append a suffix of tokens to a harmful request and optimize the suffix, by gradient over a white-box model, to maximize the probability that the model begins its answer with an affirmative phrase.

  • group relative policy optimization (GRPO) 组相对策略优化
    First occurrence: Chapter 20 · Training Models to Reason

    Where a verifier exists, it beats a reward model because it removes the learned proxy gap; group relative policy optimization (GRPO) and REINFORCE leave-one-out (RLOO) then simplify proximal policy optimization (PPO) by dropping the critic; the o1 and R1 line showed that long-horizon reasoning can emerge without step-level supervision; and the field still disagrees about whether that training teaches new reasoning or mainly reweights what the base model could already sample.

  • hardware FLOPs utilization (HFU) 硬件 FLOPs 利用率
    First occurrence: Chapter 46 · Accelerators and Networking

    Its sibling, hardware FLOPs utilization (HFU), also counts work the hardware genuinely did but the model did not strictly need, most notably the extra forward passes of activation recomputation, which is why HFU exceeds MFU whenever recomputation is on.

  • held-out set 留出集
    First occurrence: Chapter 36 · Benchmarks and Their Discontents

    A held-out set is a pipeline contract, benchmark families saturate, train/test contamination silently inflates numbers, and a score counts as evidence only when the harness, data contract, and uncertainty are visible.

  • hierarchical navigable small-world (HNSW) 分层可导航小世界图
    First occurrence: Chapter 33 · RAG and Retrieval

    The dominant structure is the hierarchical navigable small-world (HNSW) graph of Malkov and Yashunin, which builds a multi-layer proximity graph and searches it greedily from a sparse top layer down, giving logarithmic-scale query time.

  • high-bandwidth memory (HBM) 高带宽显存
    First occurrence: Chapter 46 · Accelerators and Networking

    An accelerator pairs a large matrix-multiply throughput with high-bandwidth memory (HBM) sitting next to the die.

  • Holistic Evaluation of Language Models HELM
    First occurrence: Chapter 36 · Benchmarks and Their Discontents

    This is the motivation behind standardized evaluation frameworks like Holistic Evaluation of Language Models, which pin the harness so that a score reflects the model rather than the measurement apparatus.

  • hybrid search 混合搜索
    First occurrence: Chapter 33 · RAG and Retrieval

    hybrid search answers the blind spot of dense retrieval.

  • identity preference optimization (IPO) 恒等偏好优化
    First occurrence: Chapter 17 · Direct Preference Optimization and Its Variants

    identity preference optimization (IPO) bounds the objective.

  • instruction hierarchy 指令层级
    First occurrence: Chapter 42 · Runtime Safety: Guardrails and Moderation

    The instruction hierarchy trains the model to rank its sources, system above user above tool output, and obey a lower-privilege instruction only when it aligns with a higher one; spotlighting marks untrusted spans so the model treats them as inert.

  • inter-chip interconnect (ICI) 芯片间互连
    First occurrence: Chapter 46 · Accelerators and Networking

    The chips connect through the inter-chip interconnect (ICI) and an optical circuit switch (OCS) into a torus topology rather than a switched fat-tree, so neighbor-to-neighbor bandwidth is plentiful and the sharding is co-designed with the torus shape through GSPMD-style annotations rather than mapped onto a fat-tree after the fact.

  • jailbreak 越狱
    First occurrence: Chapter 42 · Runtime Safety: Guardrails and Moderation

    Constitutional Classifiers train input and output classifiers on synthetic data generated from a written constitution of permitted and forbidden content, augmented with translations and known jailbreak transformations; the output classifier is streaming, a value head predicting harmfulness token by token so generation can be halted mid-stream.

  • Kahneman-Tversky optimization (KTO) Kahneman-Tversky 优化
    First occurrence: Chapter 17 · Direct Preference Optimization and Its Variants

    Kahneman-Tversky optimization (KTO) drops the paired-data requirement.

  • key-value cache KV 缓存
    First occurrence: Chapter 1 · The Whole Stack in One Pass

    The core tension, that generation is memory-bound and the key-value cache grows with every token, is Chapter 22.

  • knowledge editing 知识编辑
    First occurrence: Chapter 44 · Privacy, Provenance, and Unlearning

    knowledge editing attacks a narrower target: not deleting a record but changing a specific fact the model will state, for instance updating who holds an office.

  • Kullback-Leibler divergence (KL) Kullback-Leibler 散度
    First occurrence: Chapter 16 · RLHF and Reward Modeling

    The fix is a constraint: penalize the policy for drifting from the SFT reference it started at, measured as a per-token Kullback-Leibler divergence (KL).

  • least-to-most 由最少到最多
    First occurrence: Chapter 19 · Eliciting Reasoning

    least-to-most prompting asks the model first to break a problem into ordered subproblems and then to solve them in sequence, each conditioned on the solved ones, which lets the model generalize to problems harder than any single exemplar in the prompt.

  • lethal trifecta 致命三元组
    First occurrence: Chapter 42 · Runtime Safety: Guardrails and Moderation

    The precondition for catastrophe is a lethal trifecta: an agent that at once has access to private data, exposure to untrusted content, and a channel to communicate outward can be made to route the private data to an attacker, and removing any one leg breaks the exfiltration path.

  • liveness 活性
    First occurrence: Chapter 38 · Evaluating Agents and Capabilities

    One is the separation of liveness from safety: a system can fail by producing a wrong answer or by producing none, and the empirical result above is that agent-consensus failures are mostly liveness failures.

  • locality-sensitive hashing (LSH) 局部敏感哈希
    First occurrence: Chapter 5 · Data Curation and Quality

    MinHash with locality-sensitive hashing (LSH) does fuzzy document-level dedup at corpus scale: it estimates Jaccard similarity between documents from a small set of hashed shingles, so the cost is sub-quadratic rather than all-pairs.

  • low-rank adaptation (LoRA) 低秩适配
    First occurrence: Chapter 15 · Supervised Fine-Tuning and PEFT

  • machine unlearning 机器遗忘
    First occurrence: Chapter 44 · Privacy, Provenance, and Unlearning

    That is why a model leaks at serving time what it absorbed at training time, why the only sound place to fix the leak is upstream or in a retraining run rather than at the output, what machine unlearning can and cannot promise when the law demands a fact be removed, and why provenance for generated content is a separate problem from privacy even though the same controls often touch both.

  • mean time between failures (MTBF) 平均无故障时间
    First occurrence: Chapter 51 · The Machine That Breaks at Scale

    The per-device failure rate is roughly fixed by the silicon and its memory, which makes the math of large $N$ unforgiving: the mean time between failures (MTBF) for the whole job falls in proportion to the device count.

  • mechanistic interpretability 机械可解释性
    First occurrence: Chapter 39 · Mechanistic Interpretability

    mechanistic interpretability is the attempt to read those numbers as computation: to find the features a model represents and the circuits that connect them, the way one might reverse engineer a binary into source.

  • membership inference 成员推断

  • minimum description length (MDL) 最小描述长度
    First occurrence: Chapter 3 · Borrowed Ideas: What AI Took From Other Sciences

    Rissanen's minimum description length (MDL) picks the model $H$ that minimizes $L(H) + L(D \mid H)$, the bits to describe the model plus the bits to describe the data given it, which turns Occam's razor into a computable criterion: the hypothesis that compresses most has learned most.

  • mixture-of-experts (MoE) 混合专家
    First occurrence: Chapter 1 · The Whole Stack in One Pass

    Two running models ride alongside the request the whole way, chosen because they answer the same questions with opposite engineering: Llama 3, a dense Transformer, and DeepSeek-V3, a mixture-of-experts (MoE).

  • model card 模型卡

  • Model Context Protocol (MCP) 模型上下文协议

  • model-as-judge 模型作为评判者

  • Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) 蒙特卡洛树搜索

    Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) over the token stream was tried and dropped too, because token generation opens an exponentially larger search space than a board game and the step-value model it needs is hard to train.

  • multi-head latent attention (MLA) 多头潜在注意力
    First occurrence: Chapter 1 · The Whole Stack in One Pass

    DeepSeek-V3 is a mixture of experts, 671B total parameters but only 37B activated per token, paired with multi-head latent attention (MLA) to shrink the inference-time cache.

  • next-token prediction 下一词元预测

  • non-autoregressive generation (NAR) 非自回归生成
    First occurrence: Chapter 11 · Non-Autoregressive and Diffusion Language Models

    non-autoregressive generation (NAR) produces the tokens in parallel instead.

  • observability 可观测性

    observability asks "what actually happened?

  • odds-ratio preference optimization (ORPO) 优势比偏好优化

  • on-behalf-of (OBO) 代表用户

    on-behalf-of (OBO) propagates the user's authority end to end instead of replacing it with the agent's, so a warehouse's row-level security sees the human, not the service account.

  • PagedAttention PagedAttention 分页注意力

    PagedAttention and the vLLM engine borrowed virtual memory's paging from operating systems: store the KV cache in fixed-size blocks that need not be contiguous, allocate blocks on demand as a sequence grows, and let a block table map logical positions to physical blocks.

  • pass@k 至少一次成功率
